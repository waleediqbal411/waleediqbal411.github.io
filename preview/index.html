Hi, my name is

Waleed Iqbal.

I measure inequality online.

About me

Hello! I'm Waleed, and I like asking what the internet remembers about the places and people it serves.

I earned my PhD at Queen Mary University of London under Dr. Ignacio Castro and Prof. Gareth Tyson in the Social Data Science Lab. Since September 2023 I have been recognised as UK Global Talent (Exceptional Promise) in Research and Academia by the Royal Academy of Engineering.

These days I split my week between teaching data science in Aldgate, writing papers about linguistic reach and online inequality, and supervising a small handful of brave students.

Here are a few technologies and methods I've been working with recently:

  • Python
  • R & tidyverse
  • Causal inference
  • Natural language processing
  • Network analysis
  • Internet measurement

What I'm thinking about

01

Featured research

Socio-economic inequality, online

How income and geography shape which services people use, which languages a platform serves well, and whose questions a search engine actually answers. The long-running thread that holds most of my other work together.
  • Measurement
  • Causal
  • Platform data
02

Featured research

Computational social science

Methods for drawing honest social inferences from very large, very messy behavioural data. Where models stop and inference starts; what counts as evidence when the data was never collected for research.
  • Statistics
  • Simulation
  • Ethics
03

Featured research

Internet measurement

Instrumenting the open web — DNS, routing, content delivery — to understand what the infrastructure says about who it was built for. Infrastructure as evidence of value.
  • Networking
  • Observability
  • Policy
04

Featured research

Bibliometrics & science of science

How the research community itself produces, cites, and occasionally forgets its own ideas. Patterns of attention in scholarly publishing, and what they imply for who gets heard.
  • NLP
  • Networks
  • Attention
