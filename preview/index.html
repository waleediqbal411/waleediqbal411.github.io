01About

I study the shapes that inequality makes online.

I am an Assistant Professor in Data Science at Northeastern University London, and a Teaching Fellow at the School of Electronic Engineering and Computer Science, Queen Mary University of London.

My PhD, supervised by Dr. Ignacio Castro and Prof. Gareth Tyson in the Social Data Science Lab, looked at how socio-economic inequalities surface in digital traces — what people search for, who they read, how platforms route attention. The question keeps me busy.

Since 2023 I have been recognised as UK Global Talent (Exceptional Promise) in Research and Academia by the Royal Academy of Engineering.

02Research

Five threads running through the work.

Different projects, same underlying question: when the social world moves online, which inequalities travel with it, and which new ones does the medium invent?

Thread 01

Socio-economic inequality online

Measuring how income, geography, and language shape digital participation at platform scale.

Thread 02

Computational social science

Methods for drawing honest social inferences from very large, very messy behavioural data.

Thread 03

Internet measurement

Instrumenting the open web to understand infrastructure, outages, and the quiet politics of routing.

Thread 04

Bibliometrics & science of science

How the research community itself produces, cites, and sometimes forgets its own ideas.

Thread 05

Natural language processing

Language as evidence: using NLP to turn text at scale into social measurement.
