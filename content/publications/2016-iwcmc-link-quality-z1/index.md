abstract: |
  In low-power, low-rate wireless networks, IEEE 802.15.4 is a standard protocol. Devices in such
  networks are typically battery-operated; the radio transceiver is short-range yet power-hungry.
  Antenna orientation, inter-node distance, and channel selection all affect reliability. We evaluate
  IEEE 802.15.4 link performance using Zolertia Z1 motes in indoor and outdoor scenarios. RSSI
  decreases with distance and is affected by mote height and antenna polarisation; LQI and packet
  delivery ratio are largely unaffected. Non-interfering channels 26 and 15 are similarly affected
  by Wi-Fi. Contiki MAC outperforms XMAC.
